A calm, open-source browser.

Built around a sidebar, Spaces and your profiles. Chromium inside, a native macOS interface outside.

Features

Your tabs, kept in order.

A browser that organises itself around how you work, not around a row of tabs.

Vertical sidebar

Favorites, pinned tabs and today's tabs, sorted into folders.

Spaces

Separate sets of tabs, each with its own name, icon and color theme.

Profiles

Isolated cookies, history and logins. Several Spaces can share one.

Command Bar

One field for URLs, search, open tabs, history and actions.

Split View and Peek

Pages side by side. Link previews that open above the page.

Auto-archive

Inactive tabs tidy themselves away. Restore them any time.

Link routing

Links opened from other apps land in the right Space.

Extensions and import

Chrome extensions from the Chrome Web Store. Import from Arc and other browsers.

Coming from Arc?

Arc is shutting down on December 22, 2026. Arbalete keeps the ideas many of us came to rely on (a sidebar, Spaces, profiles) in an open-source browser that no single company can switch off. Import your Spaces and tabs, and carry on.

Not supported yet

What you'll miss, for now.

Arbalete is a preview. Here is what it can't do yet, so you know before you switch.

  • Streaming with DRM. Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video and similar video sites won't play. It needs Google's Widevine, which takes a license from Google.
  • Sharing Boosts. Boosts stay on the Mac they were made on: no share links, and Arc's Boosts aren't imported.
  • Sharing and importing Easels. Easels work, but an Easel can't be shared as a web page yet, and Arc's Easels aren't imported.
  • Live Folders. Folders don't fill themselves from GitHub, feeds or other sources.
  • Sync between Macs. Spaces, tabs and settings stay on the Mac they were made on.
  • Passwords and cookies on import. You sign in to your sites again. Your passwords stay in your old browser or password manager.
  • Extensions in private windows. Extensions don't run in private windows.
  • Intel Macs. Arbalete needs a Mac with Apple Silicon and macOS 14 or later.
  • Notarization by Apple. A downloaded disk image opens the first time through System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway (Homebrew skips that). After each update, macOS asks to let Arbalete use “Chromium Safe Storage” in the keychain: click Always Allow.

Built in the open

Read the code. Shape the browser.

Arbalete is written in Rust on top of Chromium through CEF, with a native SwiftUI interface. It is licensed under MPL-2.0. Issues, ideas and pull requests are welcome.
